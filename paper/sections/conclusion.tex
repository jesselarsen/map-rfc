\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agent protocols now carry tasks, discovery records and payments. They do
not yet carry accountability for the work itself. \sys{} supplies it as a
thin layer over A2A and has three parts:
\begin{itemize}
\item positive evidence that needs both parties' signatures;
\item trust that each observer computes from evidence it can verify;
\item checkpoints the requester countersigns, so work can move to another
  provider without trusting the one that failed.
\end{itemize}

Our main pre-registered trust hypothesis was that local trust at least
halves the attacker share of both a global ledger and a canary baseline.
It was only partially supported, holding in 18 of 28 settings (27 of 28
against the canary alone). Local trust survived the slander that
collapses a global ledger. For tasks whose state is their output,
countersigned resumption regenerated 0.42\% of the work that restarting
does. In an exploratory
comparison with the canary-plus-ledger design that Wang et al.\ describe,
\sys{} with the same canary was better in 25 of 35 settings, with
overlapping CIs in the other 10. The canary and \sys{}'s memory are
complementary. The evaluation also exposed where \sys{} loses: cold
start; on-off, whitewashing and camouflage relative to a global ledger;
identifier grinding; and, without a canary, whitewashing and slander
relative to that design. It showed that our own pre-registered trust
propagation collapses under Sybil-majority camouflage. That finding changed the
recommended default.

None of our negative results is new in principle. Global ledgers that
admit unilateral complaints collapse under free Sybil slander. Propagated
trust collapses once camouflaging Sybils outnumber honest raters. And when
identities are free, no shard function stops grinding. Their value here is
as measurements in the agent setting. Two of them changed the
specification: the default weighting, and epoch-salted sharding is no
longer recommended as a mitigation. The
lesson we did not expect was methodological: a
pre-registered decision rule applied at one adversarial fraction chose a
weighting that failed at another. A pre-registration should fix where a
decision is tested, as well as how it is made.

The specification, both implementations, the simulator, the
pre-registration with all amendments, and every per-seed result will be
released under the Apache-2.0 license (Appendix~\ref{app:artifact}).
