\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One author, one model family.}
One person directed \sys{}, both implementations, the baselines and the
simulator. Much of that work, and of this paper, was written with a
single AI model family (Acknowledgments). That concentrates the risk of
correlated error: the same model lineage built the system and the
baselines that test it, and it also ran the reviews of both. Those
reviews ran in separate sessions without the drafting context, but they
are not independent human review. The Go implementation was written with
the Rust source open, so it is a second implementation, not an
independent one. Our mitigations:
\begin{itemize}
\item the pre-registration;
\item replicating a third party's published curve before comparing
  against it;
\item implementing the global-ledger baseline as \sys{} minus issuer
  weighting, which makes H1 harder to meet;
\item publishing all code, configurations and per-seed CSVs.
\end{itemize}
None of these substitutes for independent replication or for review by people.

\paragraph{Simulation, not deployment.}
E1--E4 run in simulation. The world model has fixed success probabilities,
Poisson arrivals and a public receipt pool, and these are assumptions.
Real providers' quality drifts, real requesters overlap in which providers
they use more unevenly than our uniform shortlists, and real pools may be
partial. A13 tests partial pools. W1 survives them under farming and
slander, but falls behind self-only trust under camouflage once an
observer sees 20\% or less of other honest receipts. Signing is real only on a check grid (A1). The trust grid used
the fast mode, proven byte-identical on that grid. B1 charges nothing for
feedback, whereas ERC-8004's on-chain feedback costs a transaction fee. So
B1's collapse under Sybil slander is the worst case for a ledger that
charges for feedback.

\paragraph{Parts are tokens in the simulation.}
E2--E4 treat one Part as one token. A2A Parts can be much larger, such as a
file or a whole message. Then $c$ counts much larger units, which raises
the waste and the free-riding exposure per checkpoint and lowers the
relative cost of signing.

\paragraph{Decisions made after seeing data.}
Two decisions changed after results were seen:
\begin{itemize}
\item the D5 rule's repair (A4), after two-seed numbers;
\item the minimax re-test (A9), after W2's collapse;
\item the Sybil-count grid (A10), after a one-seed timing probe whose
  numbers the amendment discloses.
\end{itemize}
Both are disclosed with the original readings. The minimax rule was
pushed before its run, but it was written knowing W2's worst cell. W1's
worst cell was the new information.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Discovery capture and free identities.} Neither is solved
  at the discovery layer (E3b), and admission costs are out of scope.
  With a flooded discovery layer, only shortlist memory and a canary
  protect a requester, and a new requester has no memory (A11).
\item \textbf{Cold start.} It favours canary-style verification (L1).
\item \textbf{Behaviour changes.} On-off attackers exploit decay lag (L3).
\item \textbf{Dishonest requesters.} \sys{} has no provider-side trust
  in requesters. Free-riding is bounded per task but not across fresh
  identities, and we analyse it rather than simulate it
  (\S\ref{sec:security}).
\item \textbf{Camouflage at scale.} Direct agreement bounds each Sybil's
  weight, not how many Sybils there are. W1 falls behind self-only trust
  at 6.4 camouflaging Sybils per honest requester (A10). Camouflaged
  Sybils can also aim their weight at chosen honest providers: they cut
  those providers' traffic by 68\% under W1 (A12).
\item \textbf{Formal coverage.} We have no model of receipt or checkpoint
  non-repudiation.
\item \textbf{Keys and privacy.} Key compromise is unrecoverable within
  the layer, and receipts are pseudonymous personal data whose disclosure
  is the holder's choice.
\item \textbf{Zones} provide accountability, not confidentiality.
\item \textbf{Resumption scope.} Resuming from the output prefix does not
  recover a provider's hidden state or its external side effects, which
  a successor may repeat (\S\ref{sec:checkpoints}). E2 counts
  regenerated Parts, not the cost of ingesting the prefix.
\item \textbf{Disjoint-path lookups} are specified and simulated, but not
  yet implemented in our libp2p node.
\end{itemize}
