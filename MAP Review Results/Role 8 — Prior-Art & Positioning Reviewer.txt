## 2. Novelty — honest assessment

**The thesis is argued against the wrong opponent.** This is the single most damaging structural problem.

`draft-map-intro-01` §1 says prior attempts "contained defects that would be fatal in standards review," and `01-design-critique.md` opens: *"Documents reviewed: `design1.txt` (capability-mesh concept), `design2.txt` … `rfc_preprint.txt` (HEP v0.1 preprint)."* Those files are in `input-designs/` — and they are **chat-assistant transcripts**. `design1.txt` opens *"I actually think you're onto something interesting—but I'd avoid copying MP2P itself."* `design prompt.txt` is literally a prompt.

So C1–C15 demolish a private brainstorm. Meanwhile, **the eleven drafts cite zero actual prior art.** I grepped the entire `map-spec/` tree for A2A, Agent2Agent, MCP, AP2, DIDComm, AGNTCY, ACP, SCITT, WIMSE, GNAP, FIPA, KQML, JSON-RPC — **zero hits**. The only prior-art token anywhere is one "WebAuthn" in `draft-map-identity-01:189`.

> **R8-01 (BLOCKING).** No related-work section exists in any draft. The ISE's first question is "does this duplicate existing work?" and the documents contain no answer. A reviewer will read §1's *"Several recent design sketches and a preprint (HEP v0.1) attempted exactly this"* as either unaware of A2A/MCP/AP2/ERC-8004 or evading them.

> **R8-02 (BLOCKING).** `draft-map-arch-01` §1: *"The essential inversion, taken from the attached design work"*; `draft-map-ops-01` §7.2 is titled *"From HEP-0.1 drafts (the attached preprint)"*. **"Attached"** to what? An RFC is a standalone document. These are dangling references to unpublished chat logs. Both must be cut or the material published and cited.

Now the three claimed novelties, judged individually:

**(a) Evidence-based trust without self-asserted scores — recombination, well-executed.** The prohibition (trust §1 P1: *"Descriptors … MUST NOT carry trust, reputation, hallucination-rate, or tasks-completed fields"*) is correct and correctly motivated. But it is not new. [ERC-8004](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004) already splits Identity / Reputation / Validation registries precisely so reputation is counterparty-attested; SCITT already does signed-statement-plus-receipt; `draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts` already does Ed25519 offline-verifiable receipts; AP2 already produces dispute-grade signed evidence. **What is genuinely MAP's:** the *dual* signature with a numeric countersignature-agreement tolerance (trust §3: fields 1–4 exactly equal, usage fields within ±5% anchored to the **observed** value, timestamps ±2000 ms) and the anti-farming argument that the band is anchored to the provider's observation, not `max(claimed, observed)`. That is a real, small, publishable contribution. It is one section, not a document family.

**(b) Two-plane discovery — the strongest claim, but narrower than stated.** ADS has a "two-**level** mapping" (capability→CID, CID→peer), which is a different axis. MAP's split is stable-vs-volatile, and ADS has **no volatile plane at all** — no liveness, load, or freshness concept. MAP additionally specifies what ADS omits entirely: signed DHT records, TTL/republication (`≥1 h`, hourly), hot-key sharding, and R3 (*"Latency is measured by the querier, never advertised"*). **Verdict: genuinely differentiated from ADS.** But it is differentiated *within a space ADS already occupies with a shipping implementation and an IETF draft*, and the drafts never say so.

**(c) Four payment terms (postpaid / prepaid / escrow / metered-incremental) — recombination.** `PayQuote` key 7 enumerating these is ordinary commerce vocabulary. The three-moment discipline in pay §1 (*"**agreed** … **submitted** … **settled**. Delivery obligations key off *settled* … never off *submitted*"*) is good engineering hygiene, not novelty. **What is genuinely MAP's:** terms-3 metered-incremental with `UsageWatermark` and exact decimal arithmetic *with no rounding rule anywhere in the family*. x402 settles a fixed amount; AP2 authorizes a cart. Continuous exact metering with a covering proof on resume is a real gap in the current stack — and third-party commentary independently names metering as the unsolved piece. **This is the most publishable single idea in the family.**

**Overall: mostly recombination, with two real contributions** — the countersignature tolerance band, and exact incremental metering. Both are sections, not eleven documents. The architecture is coherent and the engineering discipline is above the median for this space; that is not the same as novelty.

## 4. The Manolito framing

Contained, and less damaging than you might fear — but net negative.

It appears in exactly six places: the protocol name, `arch` §1 ("Introduction and Heritage"), `intro` §1, the `index` abstract, `ops` §7.1, and an `arch` informative reference reading *"Manolito/MP2P historical documentation (informative)."*

The problem is not tone, it's **unverifiability and inaccuracy**. `arch` §1 asserts: *"Manolito/MP2P (c. 2001) demonstrated that a flat, serverless, UDP-centric network of consumer nodes could sustain search and exchange at scale with minimal per-node state."* MAP retains *"decentralization without supernodes."* The available record ([OpenLITO](https://openlito.sourceforge.net/)) says MANOLITO was UDP-based for search routing, **based on Gnutella**, and that hosts bootstrap via **an HTTP network gateway** returning ~100 peers. "Serverless" and "without supernodes" are doing work the citation can't support — and the citation is a bare phrase pointing at nothing.

> **R8-05 (MAJOR).** The heritage claim is load-bearing (it's the naming rationale and the framing of §1 in three documents) and rests on an uncitable informative reference to a defunct proprietary protocol reverse-engineered by hobbyists. An IETF reviewer will ask for the citation and there isn't one that supports "serverless, no supernodes."

**Recommendation:** keep the name (it's a name; RFCs have odder ones), **cut the historical argument to two sentences** in `intro` only, delete `arch` §1's technical claims about MP2P, and delete `ops` §7.1 entirely — a migration guide from a network with no remaining users, to which you explicitly disclaim wire compatibility, is pure surface area for objection. What survives is one honest line: *the architectural inversion from "who has this file" to "who can do this task."* That sentence is the good part and it doesn't need the history to stand up.

## 5. The blocking process problem — MAP cannot be published on the ISE stream as written

This is independent of technical merit and I'd put it first in any cover note.

[RFC 8726](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8726.html) §4: *"In general, documents on the Independent Stream cannot request the creation of a new IANA registry."* The sole exception is a sub-code registry tied to one code point from an existing registry, and *"Such examples are rare."*

§2: *"a registry whose policy is 'IETF Review' or 'Standards Action' [RFC8126] is not available to Independent Stream documents."*

§5, three consecutive sentences: *"The ISE will not appoint a DE. That means that no subregistry created for Independent Stream documents will require the review of a DE. That means that no new subregistry can be created that uses the Specification Required or Expert Review policies."*

And the ISE's [own criteria page](https://www.rfc-editor.org/about/independent/): *"they must adhere to any IANA rules for code point allocation, and in general may not create new IANA registries"* — plus *"nor are foundational formats upon which standards are expected to be built."*

Against that, `draft-map-wire-01` §4 requests **~20 new registries** across exactly the three policies that are unavailable:

- **Standards Action** — Cryptographic Suites, AgentID Key Types → categorically barred, twice over
- **Specification Required** — Message Types, Body Keys, Error Codes, Extension Keys, Transport Bindings, Settlement Methods, Pricing Models, Terms, Unit Codes, Dispute codes/kinds → barred (no DE)
- **Expert Review** — Capability Ontology, Delegation Caveats, Chunk Kinds, HW/Sandbox Classes, Revocation Reasons, Task Caveat Keys, CapEntry Parameter Keys, Trust Evidence/Vouch/Challenge → barred (no DE)

> **R8-06 (BLOCKING).** Every MAP registry uses a policy the ISE cannot grant. `draft-map-ops-01` §3.5 asserts *"On IETF publication, **IANA** operates every registry under the policies enumerated in `draft-map-wire-01.md` §4"* and *"the designated experts become IANA-appointed Expert Reviewers."* Per RFC 8726 §5 this will not happen on this stream. `SUBMISSION.md`'s "MUST resolve" list names the **category** conflict (std/bcp vs Informational) but **not the registry conflict**, which is the larger of the two and cannot be fixed by relabeling.

> **R8-07 (MAJOR).** `ops` §3.5 also says *"the Independent Stream shepherd and the IESG provide the oversight the stream defines."* The IESG does not oversee the Independent Stream; it performs conflict review only, under [RFC 5742](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5742). Oversight is the ISE's. Small error, but it is in the one section claiming to fix governance, and it signals unfamiliarity with the stream being targeted.

**RFC 8726 §4 also names the way out:** *"Authors … should consider the most appropriate venue to host such registries … In some cases, this may mean that registries are hosted by organizations other than IANA."* That is your answer — run the registries yourself (you already specify a steward in ops §3.5) and cite them informatively.

**Conflict review timing.** `agentproto` is on the **2026-10-08 IESG telechat**. Its charter deliberately excludes discovery, says nothing about payment, treats trust only as "trust boundaries," and never mentions decentralized operation — so a genuine RFC 5742 *conflict* is unlikely. But the overlap is not zero on the session/dialog-identifier axis, and the IESG may request a delay: RFC 4846 permits *"a delay of up to six months and, if necessary, to renew that request twice, for a total possible delay of 18 months."* Submitting an 11-document family in the same quarter a WG charters in the adjacent space invites exactly that.

## 7. Recommendation

**Publish-subset — but not on the path currently planned, and not this quarter.**

The full family fails RFC 4846's relevance test not on quality but on posture: it is a complete standards-track protocol suite, and the ISE page explicitly excludes *"foundational formats upon which standards are expected to be built."* Eleven documents, ~20 registries, and a conformance-class system is a working group's output submitted to a venue that publishes individual contributions. It will not survive Step 4 of the ISE's own twelve-step process.

**Submit two documents, both Informational, both registry-free:**

1. **`draft-map-arch-01`**, reworked — goals, threat model TH1–TH15, the zone model, and the scalability analysis, presented as *"an architecture for decentralized agent capability networks"* with a real related-work section. The zone model (Zones 0–3 gating where payloads may flow) is the piece an IETF reader will find immediately useful and nobody else in the table above specifies it. Strip the Manolito technical claims; cut `ops` §7.1.

2. **`draft-map-pay-01`, reduced to the metering contribution** — the three-moment discipline (agreed/submitted/settled), `UsageWatermark`, exact decimal arithmetic with no rounding, and metered pause/resume. Drop the settlement-method registry (that's the registry problem in miniature), drop the `pay:x402` method definition (stale, and it belongs in a versioned registry not an RFC), keep x402/AP2/Lightning as informative examples of rails this layers over. **This is your strongest and most differentiated material**, and it lands in a genuine gap: AP2 authorizes a cart, x402 settles a fixed amount, and nothing standardizes continuous exact metering.

**Hold everything else.** `core`, `identity`, `discovery`, `tasks`, `trust`, `wire`, `ops` are the registry-bearing documents and the ones most exposed to `agentproto`, A2A v1.0, and ADS. `intro` and `index` are scaffolding for a family that isn't being submitted; fold what's needed into the arch document. `01-design-critique.md` should never be submitted or cited in a submitted draft — it critiques private chat transcripts, and citing it is what makes R8-01 and R8-02 unavoidable.

**Before either document goes anywhere:**
1. Write the related-work section. It's the ISE's core question and you currently have no answer. (R8-01)
2. Remove every "attached" reference. (R8-02)
3. Author real references sections. (R8-04)
4. Fix the independence claim. (R8-08)
5. Wait for the 2026-10-08 telechat outcome on `agentproto`, then position explicitly against the chartered scope.

**On technical soundness — RFC 4846's second test:** it passes, and by a margin. Both implementations build and test green, 26/26 live interop is real and I reproduced it, the vectors are byte-exact, the deterministic-CBOR/verify-received-bytes discipline is right, and the engineering-policy section is more rigorous than most WG output. The objections above are about *positioning, provenance, and process* — not about whether the protocol works. That's the frustrating part: the work is better than its framing, and the framing is what will sink it.
