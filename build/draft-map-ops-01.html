MAP Operations: Governance, Registries, Federation, Interoperability, and Migration

Abstract

This document covers the operational half of the MAP (Manolito Agent Protocol) family: how the specifications are governed and changed, how their registries are administered, how independently operated meshes federate, how conformance is demonstrated, and how deployments migrate.¶

It defines the engineering and data-semantics policy the family is written against; the registration policy, change control and stewardship arrangements for the code-point registries indexed in draft-map-wire-01; the rules governing federation gateways, including the admission, attribution and bounded-spend constraints a gateway must satisfy to bridge two meshes or to front a paid external API; the conformance program — test vectors, negative vectors, the pairwise interoperability matrix, and the formal-analysis effort, together with the advancement rule that gates a document's maturity on two implementations demonstrating interoperation; and guidance for implementers and for migration from predecessor designs.¶

This document specifies no wire format and assigns no code points.¶

2. Scope

Everything that keeps the MAP family coherent over time: the engineering policy binding the documents together, code-point registries and their change policies, the deprecation process, federation between meshes, conformance and interoperability testing, the formal-analysis program, reference-implementation guidance, migration from legacy designs, and regulatory notes.¶

3. Engineering policy: bounded contexts and data semantics

The document family is a deliberate bounded-context decomposition. Each document owns a vocabulary, a small set of aggregates, and explicit data semantics; cross-context references are by identity only (AgentID, TaskID, quote-id, invoice-id, descriptor-cid, transcript-root), never by embedded foreign structures. Context integration happens through signed messages and translation (gateways, §5), never shared internal models.¶

Binding rules across the family (restating what each document enforces locally, so reviewers can check any change against one list):¶

  1. One source of truth per fact, named explicitly; everything else is¶

a derived copy with a repair path (republish, re-fetch, recompute).¶

  1. Events are past-tense facts (RECEIPT, PAY_RECEIPT, REVOKE);¶

commands are requests (ASSIGN, CANCEL, PAY_AGREE); the two are never conflated in naming or semantics.¶

  1. Idempotency everywhere delivery is uncertain: TaskID, invoice-id,¶

msg-id, content addresses. No handler MAY have unrepeatable side effects; no design MAY assume exactly-once delivery.¶

  1. Ordering is scoped, never global — and each scope is written down.¶
  2. Distinguish accepted / durable / applied (visible in pay §1's¶

agreed/submitted/settled and tasks' countersigned-checkpoint boundary).¶

  1. Schema evolution is additive within a major version; contract¶

changes ride registries and version negotiation; fields are never reused with new meaning (registry policy forbids re-assignment, §3.3).¶

  1. Projections MUST be rebuildable (trust scores from receipts, DHT¶

index from descriptors) and their staleness observable.¶

Changes to any MAP document that violate a row or rule above MUST be rejected in review or accompanied by an explicit amendment to this table.¶

4. Registries and change control

Registries are documents plus process — never runtime services (arch G1).¶

4.1. Designated experts

Two or more per registry, appointed by the project's steering process; review criteria: no semantic overlap with existing points, complete specification, security considerations present.¶

4.2. Private/experimental use

Private ranges (core §5, ext keys ≥ 0xF000) require no registration and MUST NOT appear in public-mesh descriptors.¶

4.3. Immutability of assignments

Code points are never reused or re-defined; deprecation marks them reserved forever (rule 6 above). As a standing example, message type 0x0206 is a permanent, deliberately-unassigned reservation in the Tasks range (wire §3.20): it is never reused for any future meaning, and any document or implementation that claims 0x0206 is non-conformant.¶

4.4. Deprecation process

Mark deprecated in registry with rationale and replacement → minimum two minor versions of dual support → removal only at a major version, with the major's migration notes. HELLO negotiation makes the sunset observable in the field (peers advertising deprecated versions are countable).¶

4.5. Registry stewardship and IETF submission

The MAP family is intended for publication as Independent Stream Internet-Drafts (each draft's submissiontype: independent front-matter records this). This section fixes who owns the registries and how governance works — both before and after publication — so the family is externally submittable without an undefined "steering process".¶

  • Pre-publication ownership. While the documents are Internet-Drafts (not¶

yet RFCs), the protocol steward — the document editor(s) named on the front matter of this operations document — is the registry authority. The steward appoints the designated experts required by §3.1, resolves registry disputes, and runs the change-control process in §3/§4. "The project's steering process" (§3.1) means this steward appointment, not a separate body.¶

  • Post-publication ownership. On IETF publication, IANA operates every¶

registry under the policies enumerated in draft-map-wire-01.md §4 (Specification Required, Expert Review, Standards Action). The designated experts become IANA-appointed Expert Reviewers / registration reviewers per the relevant IANA policy; the Independent Stream shepherd and the IESG provide the oversight the stream defines. The MAP steward's pre-publication appointments SHOULD be carried forward as IANA's initial expert roster.¶

  • Authority split (no double authority). Internal ops §3/§4 change-control¶

governs draft revisions and the working tree. Once an RFC is published, code-point assignments are made by IANA under the registered policy; the steward no longer unilaterally assigns points. The immutability and deprecation rules (§3.3/§3.4) apply identically in both periods — IANA honors "never reuse" as a permanent reservation.¶

  • Intellectual property. The source working tree is distributed under¶

Apache-2.0 (see repository README). Contributors SHOULD disclose relevant IPR per IETF BCP 79. The published RFC is copyrighted by the IETF Trust and licensed for use per the IETF Trust's terms; this is distinct from the source-repo Apache-2.0 grant and does not change the wire format or code points.¶

  • Readiness gate. Submission (and any request that IANA create the¶

registries) is gated on the two-independent-implementation criterion in §6.1; the documents remain in draft pending human/expert review until that gate is met and the Independent Stream review completes.¶

5. Versioning discipline

Semantics per core §6.4. Additionally: every published draft revision carries a changelog distinguishing editorial / additive / breaking; breaking items MUST name the major version that will carry them. Reference implementations MUST keep a compatibility test pinning the previous minor's golden vectors.¶

6. Federation

A mesh is a deployment sharing bootstrap and policy (arch §3). Meshes federate through gateways — ordinary agents advertising map:cap:gateway/<namespace> — which are, precisely, anti-corruption layers: they translate a foreign context (another mesh, or an external service such as a hosted-model API) into local MAP vocabulary without letting the foreign model leak through.¶

Gateway rules:¶

  • G-1: A gateway signs everything as itself. It MUST NOT fabricate¶

descriptors, receipts, or proofs purporting to originate from the external party; provenance (gateway-of: <namespace> ext field) is REQUIRED on re-advertised capabilities.¶

  • G-2: Evidence about work done through a gateway accrues to the gateway's¶

AgentID — it is accountable for its upstream, which is exactly the incentive alignment wanted from the "OpenRouter gateway peer" pattern (design3, adopted).¶

  • G-3: Zone labels never launder through a gateway (arch Z2); a gateway¶

bridging to an external API is Zone ≥2 by definition unless the deployment allowlists it into Zone 1.¶

  • G-4: Namespaces are DNS-anchored (gateway/openrouter.example.org) or¶

mesh-registered; collisions resolve in favor of the DNS anchor.¶

Cross-mesh identity, receipts, and payments need no translation — they are already self-certifying — so federation cost is limited to discovery bridging and policy reconciliation.¶

6.1. Paid-API gateways (the x402 composition)

A gateway MAY front an external HTTP service that prices its requests with x402 (pay §5.1: HTTP 402 + X-PAYMENT header; the loop is appropriate exactly here, at the gateway boundary — never between MAP peers). Such a gateway participates in two independent payment legs:¶

  • Downstream (MAP leg): the gateway is an ordinary MAP payee. It¶

quotes, agrees, invoices, and settles under pay §2–§5 with any method and terms it chooses; its price is local policy (a margin over its upstream cost is typical and is nobody else's business — C5).¶

  • Upstream (rail leg): the gateway is an x402 HTTP payer, spending¶

its own funds from its own wallet against the external service's 402 challenge.¶

Rules (extending G-1..G-4 to money):¶

  • G-5 — payment legs never launder. The gateway settles each leg in¶

its own name and MUST NOT re-present one leg's artifacts as the other's: an upstream x402 settlement is not a MAP PayProof, and a MAP PayProof is never forwarded upstream. Upstream payment evidence (settlement response, tx ref) MAY be embedded in the gateway's signed transcript as gateway-attested evidence — provenance G-1 applies: it is the gateway's statement, not the upstream's.¶

  • G-6 — spend order. The gateway MUST admit the task (G-3/scope) and¶

SHOULD hold its downstream settlement (RECOMMENDED: terms 1 prepaid, pay §5.2.2 — the execution gate is exactly the credit-risk boundary) before initiating any upstream payment. A gateway MUST NOT spend upstream on a task it has rejected or will reject.¶

  • G-7 — bounded upstream spend. The gateway MUST enforce a per-task¶

ceiling on upstream payment; a 402 challenge demanding more is refused fail-closed (the task fails; no payment is made). Upstream price changes never propagate implicitly into an already-agreed MAP quote — the quote is binding (pay §3); the margin absorbs or the gateway eats the loss and reprices future quotes.¶

  • Retry safety: upstream authorizations SHOULD be derived¶

deterministically per (task, attempt) so an HTTP retry cannot double-pay (the same single-use authorization re-presents; the rail's nonce rule dedups) — the same construction as the pay §5.1 invoice nonce, under a distinct domain string.¶

Compliance (§9) attaches to the gateway as rail participant on the upstream leg and as method operator counterparty on the downstream leg; neither is compliance-free.¶

7. Conformance and interoperability testing

7.1. Structure

Per conformance class (arch §10): golden test vectors (encode/decode/ verify — TV-001/002 seed the set), negative vectors, state-machine conformance suites, and live pairwise interop matrices. Advancement rule: no document leaves draft status without two independent implementations passing the full matrix against each other.¶

Status (2026-07-17): the live pairwise matrix is delivered — 26/26 (16 core + 6 task + 4 pay), Rust (map-rs) ↔ Go (map-go). Both implementations complete real MAP sessions against each other over framed TCP, in both orientations (each side as dialer and as listener), across 8 scenarios: echo, replay (1003), sequence gap (1003), tamper (2000), cross-session relay (2000 — the live §7.3 anti-relay property), version-disjoint handshake (1001), foreign-SessionID rejection (1006, core §6.1), and delegated handshake with accountable-root resolution (identity §4/§6). The record, toolchain versions, and reproduction driver are in interop/MATRIX.md. Cross-language task flows (3 scenarios) and payment flows (2 scenarios: postpaid and metered-incremental) layer on the same session (I2) and are also live, over libp2p/QUIC, in both orientations.¶

Two qualifications on the advancement rule. First, "two independent implementations" is met mechanically and not substantively: both are the work of one author, the Go implementation written from the drafts and frozen vectors under a rule forbidding it to read Rust source. That discipline is real — it forces two readings of the same text — but it is not two parties. Second, the matrix covers the mechanisms it exercises and no others; prepaid and escrow terms, the dispute protocol (pay §6), and the x402 rail have one implementation, and several registered message types have no codec in either. A document whose mechanisms are not in the matrix has not met this rule.¶

7.2. Required negative tests (minimum)

Duplicate delivery of every idempotent message (ASSIGN, RECEIPT, PAY_INVOICE, PAY_PROOF) — observable effect exactly once. Replayed envelopes within and across sessions (MUST fail 1003; cross-session MUST fail channel binding). Out-of-order and gapped seq/chunk-seq. Truncated/duplicate-key/indefinite-length CBOR (MUST fail 1000). Stale descriptor seq (MUST NOT regress). Wrong-shard provider records (MUST be discarded). Delegation chains that widen scope/zone/depth (MUST fail 2003). Signature over re-serialized rather than received bytes (vector constructed to differ — MUST fail). Projection rebuild: delete trust scores, recompute from receipt store, byte-compare decisions; same for DHT index from descriptors.¶

7.3. Formal analysis program

Status. ProVerif 2.05 models at formal/ cover session establishment and delegation. hello-cb.pv checks that the initiator's negotiated version is the mutual maximum given the initiator-side recheck implemented in map-session, and that the channel-binding value depends on the handshake transcript; delegation.pv checks that a two-link chain rooted at an honest issuer is accepted only at the scope the root granted, over a fixed three-element capability lattice. The adversary has full active Dolev-Yao network control plus one compromised insider key. No counterexample was found in the honest models, and the downgrade, relay and delegation-widening negative controls each fail with a witnessing trace.¶

These models do not establish injective anti-relay (the HELLO_ACK freshness needed for that is present in the protocol but was not modeled), do not verify the transcript-hash mechanism of core §6.2 itself, and do not cover the fail-closed caveat rule, chain-link binding, validity windows or depth limits of identity §4. The escrow, dispute and metering models under formal/ (pay-auth.pv, pay-dispute.pv, pay-meter.pv) are drafts whose reachability guards fail — their properties are proved over an empty trace set — and are not relied on for any claim in this document.¶

Remaining formal work: a sound injective anti-relay property (one responder nonce plus a query change), a real agreement property for negotiation, receipts non-repudiation, the payment binding chain (quote→agree→invoice→proof) rebuilt as a state model, metering arithmetic (which needs bounded enumeration or a tool with natural-number reasoning), reservation obligations, and the msg-id replay cache itself. Full methodology, the property-to-spec mapping and the abstraction gaps are in formal/README.md; captured tool output is under formal/results/. Claims not covered by models or tests are labeled assumptions (arch §6).¶

7.4. Fuzzing and load

Continuous structure-aware CBOR fuzzing of every decoder; churn simulation (30 %/hour peer turnover) against discovery; hot-key load tests validating the §6-discovery sharding math at 10^5 synthetic providers per capability.¶

8. Migration

8.1. From legacy Manolito/MP2P (conceptual)

No wire compatibility (arch non-goal). Concept map: file-hash search → capability-key lookup; flat UDP peer exchange → Kademlia + AutoNAT/relay; unsigned cleartext → signed CBOR over encrypted transports; "who has the file" results → descriptors + status. The retained virtues are architectural: no supernodes, tolerance of churn, small messages.¶

9. Reference implementation guidance

Rust first (rust-libp2p maturity), Go second; workspace mirrors the context map — one crate per bounded context, dependencies only along the document dependency graph, no shared "common domain" crate beyond identifier newtypes:¶

map/            crates: map-identity, map-core, map-discovery,
                map-tasks, map-trust, map-pay
adapters/       ollama-provider, gateway (hosted-API bridge), ide-rpc
storage/        append-only receipt log + rebuildable projections;
                descriptor cache keyed (AgentID, seq)
conformance/    golden vectors, negative vectors, matrix harness
¶

Implementation musts drawn from §2: value types for AgentID/TaskID/Amount (no bare byte-strings crossing APIs); receipt ingestion idempotent; projection rebuild as a first-class CLI operation; observability for lag (descriptor staleness, credit exhaustion, retry counts, dispute rates). MVP ordering (adapted from design3's scope, which was sound): core + identity handshake → descriptor publish/fetch → echo task with streaming and flow credit → receipts → payments last.¶

The reference-implementation program is executed through implementation-specification packages in the repository's tasks/ directory (see tasks/README.md): an architect role authors complete, self-contained engineering specifications; coding agents implement them; each package carries its own acceptance criteria and validation checklist, and the spec-feedback rule of §2 (patch the spec in the same change when implementation exposes ambiguity) binds every package. The Rust workspace map-rs/ (impl #1 toward the §6.1 two-implementation rule) is the first target of this process.¶

Status (TASK-001..003): the map-rs/ scaffold builds and its full test suite is green. The conformance/map-conformance crate reproduces TV-001 and TV-002 byte-for-byte from the seeds and rejects the §6.2 negative catalog (N1–N10), satisfying the §6.2 minimum negative suite for impl #1; the deterministic-CBOR encoder reproduced the cbor2-canonical vectors without modification. Adapter seams (ollama-provider, gateway) and the map-cli tool (keygen, projection rebuild) exist; live transport, delegation, and payments remain future work per the MVP ordering above.¶

Status (TASK-015, 2026-07-11): impl #2 is at frozen-vector parity. map-go/ (Go, zero external deps, written from the drafts and vectors under the independence rule — no Rust source consulted) reproduces every frozen positive vector byte-for-byte from seeds (TV-001..015 incl. TV-010b; TV-007 ASSIGN is produce+consume in Go, closing that ERRATA parity hole on the Go side), consumes them from frozen bytes, and rejects NV-001..008 with the required classes, including the §6.2 canonicality-vs-signature isolation case (NV-003) and scope-widening delegation (NV-007 → 2003). Strict Ed25519 verification (canonical A/R/S, small-order rejection) is implemented and tested per TASK-015 §FR-4. The live pairwise interop MATRIX (two implementations against each other over a real transport) remains future work — vector parity alone does not advance any document out of draft.¶

10. Compliance and regulatory notes (informative)

Receipts and descriptors are pseudonymous personal data under GDPR-like regimes: holders are controllers of their local stores; the protocol compels no publication, and erasure is satisfiable because disclosure is pull-based and local deletion is always possible (trust §4). Payment compliance (KYC/AML/sanctions) attaches to settlement methods and their operators (pay §7). Model-output liability and content policy are application-layer; SecurityProfile and zones give deployments the enforcement hooks. Export-control considerations for cryptography follow the chosen suite's primitives, all of which are widely published standards.¶

11. Security Considerations

Governance capture is the meta-risk: registries use open policies with appealable expert review; no registry is a runtime dependency, so capture degrades coordination, not operation. Federation gateways concentrate trust by construction — G-1/G-2 make that concentration visible and priced rather than hidden.¶

12. IANA Considerations

This document defines the registration policies and change-control model for the MAP registries (§3), but requests no IANA action of its own: the registries and their initial contents are defined by the protocol documents, and the consolidated index is draft-map-wire-01. Should the family be adopted onto a stream that uses the IANA registries, the policies in §3 (open / Specification Required, with appealable expert review) are the requested assignment policies for each registry named there.¶
