   MAP Introduction and Motivation: Autonomous Agent Interoperability
                           draft-map-intro-01

Abstract

   The Manolito Agent Protocol (MAP) is a decentralized, permissionless
   protocol for autonomous AI agents to discover one another by
   capability, negotiate work, execute it with verifiable evidence, and
   settle payment — without a central authority, global reputation
   service, or native token.  This document states the problem MAP
   addresses, why prior designs failed, the design responses MAP adopts,
   and a reading guide for the rest of the specification family.  The
   substantive specifications are the companion draft-map-*-01
   documents; the critical evaluation that motivates them is 01-design-
   critique.md.

2.  The Problem

   Autonomous AI agents increasingly need to cooperate: one agent
   commissions another to run inference, call a tool, retrieve memory,
   or transform data.  That cooperation should be *verifiable,
   permissionless, and attriburable* — any agent should be able to find
   a capable counterparty, agree on terms, confirm the work was done,
   and pay for it, without trusting a central broker with the identity
   graph, the reputation ledger, or the money supply.

   The historical Manolito/MP2P file-sharing network (c. 2001) showed
   that a flat, serverless, high-churn peer network can route by _what
   is wanted_ rather than _where it lives_. MAP evolves that instinct
   from "who has this file?" to "who can do this task?": the network
   becomes a *capability graph*. Several recent design sketches and a
   preprint (HEP v0.1) attempted exactly this, but — as 01-design-
   critique.md documents in detail — they contained defects that would
   be fatal in standards review: self-referential signature definitions,
   self-asserted trust scores, volatile data pushed through a global

3.  Why Prior Designs Failed (high level)

   The critique identifies fifteen findings (C1–C15).  The most
   consequential:

   *  *C1 — Self-asserted trust.* Advertised trust/reputation scores are

   signed by the subject, proving only self-praise; a rational attacker
   advertises a perfect score.  Any routing formula weighted by such a
   score is steered by attacker-controlled input.

   *  *C2 — Non-canonical signatures.* Signing Protobuf structures
      (which have

   no canonical encoding) makes "serialize and verify" fail across
   implementations and enables re-serialization malleability.

   *  *C3 — Volatile state in the DHT.* Republishing load/latency every
      few

   seconds does not scale and routes on stale fiction.

   *  *C4 — No replay protection.* Signed requests can be replayed
      verbatim

   (re-executing paid work, double-spending receipts).

   *  *C5 — Normative magic-weight routing.* Fixed score weights freeze
      bad

   constants into the standard and mix attacker-controlled inputs with
   honest ones; ranking should be local policy.

   *  *C6 — Latency as a global field.* Latency is a property of a path

   between two peers, not of a node.

   *  *C7 — Privacy leakage.* Exact hardware fingerprints and broadcast
      query

   intent link a stable identity to high-entropy metadata.

   *  *C8 — Transport lock-in.* Hard-wiring gRPC/QUIC with two parallel

   identities contradicts transport-agnosticism.

   *  *C9 — Central-bank economics.* "Credits minted by the
      orchestrator"

   reintroduces the central authority the goals forbid.

   *  *C10 — Impractical resumption.* KV-cache transfer assumes
      identical

   engines; normative resumption must be engine-independent.

   *  *C11 — Checkpoint trust.* A checkpoint signed only by the party
      that may

   have failed can poison its successor.

   *  *C12 — Taxonomy anarchy.* Free-text capability strings fragment
      the

   keyspace.

   *  *C13 — Identifier fragmentation.* Three incompatible ID spaces for
      the

   same key.

   *  *C14 — Weakened requirements language.* "RFC 2119, adapted" guts

   normative force.

   *  *C15 — Sybil hand-waving.* "Reputation decay fixes Sybil" is an
      assertion,

   not a mechanism.

   The full findings, dispositions, and per-document assessment are in
   01-design-critique.md.

5.  What MAP Is Not

   To bound scope, MAP explicitly is *not* (see arch §4.1):

   *  A model-training or fine-tuning coordinator.

   *  A general-purpose file-sharing network (content transfer exists
      only in

   service of tasks).

   *  A native token, blockchain, or consensus layer.

   *  A global, objective reputation service.

   *  A guarantor of the _semantic quality_ of AI outputs — MAP makes
      behavior

   _attributable and evidence-bearing_, not correct.

   *  Wire-compatible with legacy Manolito clients (a conceptual
      migration guide

   is in ops §7).

   MAP is a *protocol, not a product*. "Hermes" and similar
   orchestrators are implementations that speak MAP; nothing in these
   documents privileges any implementation.

6.  Document Map and Reading Order

   The family comprises the documents listed in the README "Contents"
   table and the arch §11 document map.  For orientation, read in this
   order:

   1.  This introduction (draft-map-intro-01) — motivation and reading
       guide.

   2.  01-design-critique.md — the critical evaluation that justifies
       every

   design choice (findings C1–C15).

   1.  draft-map-arch-01 — goals/non-goals, threat model TH1–TH15,
       zones,

   scalability, conformance classes.

   1.  draft-map-identity-01 then draft-map-core-01 for the foundation.

   2.  Then draft-map-discovery-01 and draft-map-tasks-01.

   3.  Finally draft-map-trust-01 and draft-map-pay-01; draft-map-ops-01

   governs the family.

   For the complete wire format and the full code-point space in one
   place, consult draft-map-wire-01 (the master registry index).

   Implement in the order: identity, core, discovery, tasks, then trust/
   pay.

7.  Status and Maturity

   Two implementations by the same author under a source-isolation rule
   — map-rs (Rust) and map-go (Go), the latter written from the drafts
   and the frozen vectors without reading Rust source — reproduce every
   frozen test vector byte-for-byte and complete a live pairwise interop
   matrix (26/26 scenarios: 16 core, 6 task, 4 pay; interop/MATRIX.md)
   over real transports, in both orientations.  This meets the ops §6.1
   criterion mechanically but not substantively: two implementations by
   different parties would be stronger evidence, and that is not what
   this is.  Two session-layer properties (negotiation recheck,
   transcript-dependence of the channel binding) and one delegation
   property (scope attenuation over a fixed lattice) have ProVerif
   models with negative controls; see ops §6.3 for exactly what each
   establishes and what it does not.  Remaining formal work and the
